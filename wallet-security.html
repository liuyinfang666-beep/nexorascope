WALLET SECURITY GUIDE

Crypto Wallet Security: A Practical Guide

Build safer habits for self-custody and exchange accounts: protect recovery information, verify wallet software, understand transaction prompts, and review permissions.

Independent educational guide · Last reviewed November 17, 2026

In this guide: Recovery phrases · Wallet software · Transactions and signatures · Devices and accounts · Security routine · If compromise is suspected

1. Protect the recovery phrase and private keys

A self-custody wallet uses cryptographic keys to authorize activity. A recovery phrase (sometimes called a seed phrase) can restore access to the wallet and its associated accounts. Anyone who obtains it may be able to control the assets. Treat it like the master key to the wallet.

  • Never share a recovery phrase or private key with a person, website, support agent, form, or chatbot.
  • Do not store it in cloud notes, email drafts, chat messages, screenshots, or an unencrypted document.
  • Keep any offline backup physically secure and protected from loss, theft, fire, and water according to your circumstances.
  • Do not type the phrase into a site just to “verify,” “sync,” “validate,” or “unlock” a wallet.
  • Be careful when creating backups: verify the wallet provider’s official recovery instructions and avoid exposing the phrase to cameras or other people.

Custodial exchange accounts work differently: the provider controls the keys on your behalf, while you protect account credentials and recovery methods. Custody convenience does not remove platform, account-takeover, insolvency, or withdrawal risks. See Investor.gov’s crypto asset custody overview.

2. Verify wallet apps, browser extensions, and updates

Fake wallet apps and browser extensions can imitate familiar brands. Start from the wallet provider’s independently verified official website, then follow its download links. Check the publisher, spelling, domain, permissions requested, and update history. Do not assume a search advertisement or a social-media link is genuine.

  • Install only the software you need and remove extensions you no longer use.
  • Keep your operating system, browser, password manager, and wallet app updated.
  • Review extension permissions and be cautious if a wallet extension asks for unrelated access.
  • Avoid cracked software, unofficial wallet builds, and files sent by strangers.
  • Use a separate browser profile or device for higher-value activity if that fits your threat model.

3. Pause before signing a transaction or message

A wallet prompt is a request to authorize an action. It may be a token transfer, contract interaction, token approval, NFT operator permission, or message signature. The fact that a prompt does not display a familiar “send” action does not make it harmless.

Before approving, ask:

  1. Did I intentionally open this application, and is the domain correct?
  2. Which network and account are active?
  3. What contract, recipient, or spender is involved?
  4. What asset or permission is affected, and is the amount or scope expected?
  5. Can I understand the wallet’s transaction preview? If not, can I stop and check official documentation first?

Never sign a transaction because a countdown, support agent, or pop-up says your funds will otherwise be frozen. If the details are unclear, reject the request and independently verify the application.

4. Understand the difference between disconnecting and revoking

Disconnecting a website from a wallet interface usually removes that site’s current connection or session. It does not necessarily cancel an on-chain token allowance or NFT operator approval that was previously granted. Those permissions are recorded by the relevant smart contract and must be reviewed separately. Learn more in Understanding Token Approvals.

5. Secure the accounts and devices around your wallet

  • Use long, unique passwords for email, exchange, and wallet-related accounts; consider a reputable password manager.
  • Enable multifactor authentication or passkeys where supported. Prefer phishing-resistant methods when available.
  • Protect the email account used for password resets and exchange notices.
  • Review active sessions, trusted devices, API keys, withdrawal allowlists, and recovery contacts.
  • Use device screen locks and keep security updates enabled.
  • Do not install remote-access software or share your screen because an unsolicited “support” contact asks you to.

These are general account-security practices, not a guarantee against compromise. CISA’s Secure Our World guidance covers phishing, strong passwords, MFA, and software updates.

6. Use a repeatable security routine

A simple recurring review is easier to follow than a one-time complicated setup. Adapt this checklist to the wallet and services you actually use:

  • Confirm wallet apps and browser extensions are genuine and up to date.
  • Check that recovery information remains accessible to you but is not exposed online.
  • Review account sessions and security alerts.
  • Review token allowances and NFT operator permissions on networks you use.
  • Remove unused applications and revoke permissions you no longer need, after verifying the exact contract and transaction.
  • Keep a private record of wallet addresses and recovery procedures that does not contain secret credentials.

7. If you suspect a wallet or device is compromised

Stop interacting with the suspicious site or software. Do not enter your recovery phrase into a “rescue” page or contact details supplied by the suspected attacker. From a device you trust, use the wallet provider’s official guidance to assess next steps. If a recovery phrase or private key may have been exposed, assume the associated wallet may be compromised; moving remaining assets to a newly created secure wallet may be appropriate, but take care not to reuse the exposed secret. If you are unsure, seek help from a reputable security professional without sharing secrets.

Preserve relevant transaction hashes, URLs, timestamps, and messages for reporting. Be alert for follow-up recovery scams that promise guaranteed reimbursement for an upfront fee.

Frequently asked questions

Should I ever share my recovery phrase with wallet support?

No. A legitimate wallet provider or support agent should not need your recovery phrase or private key. Anyone asking for it should be treated as suspicious.

Does disconnecting a wallet revoke token approvals?

Not necessarily. Disconnecting usually affects a website connection. Token allowances and NFT operator approvals are separate on-chain permissions and must be reviewed independently.

Is a hardware wallet completely safe?

No device eliminates all risk. A hardware wallet can keep keys isolated from an internet-connected computer, but users still need to verify addresses, transaction details, firmware sources, backups, and physical security.

Can a wallet security checklist guarantee my assets are safe?

No. A checklist can reduce common risks but cannot guarantee safety or detect every malicious contract, compromised device, or social-engineering attempt.
